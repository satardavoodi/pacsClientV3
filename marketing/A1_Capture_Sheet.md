# A1 Teaser — AI-PACS Screen-Capture Sheet (Camtasia)

**Goal:** record the two **real AI-PACS** beats that sit in the middle of the 15s teaser (3–12s), to composite between the Higgsfield intro and outro clips.
**Tool:** Camtasia 2024 (installed). **Patient:** anonymized/demo only (see §1). **Final aspect:** 9:16 (record landscape, reframe in edit — §3).

---

## 1. Before you record — anonymization & clean-up (mandatory)
- **Use a demo/teaching patient** with non-identifying data **or** plan to blur identifiers in edit. Never publish real name/ID/age/sex/accession.
- For the **3D beat, use a body CT (abdomen/chest) — NOT the head/face CT** (a 3D face is identifiable). The abdomen "Tissue/Bone" series gives a great non-identifying 3D. If you must use a head CT, switch the 3D to a **bone/skull** look or deface it.
- You'll still blur in edit: the **corner overlays** (name/ID/age/sex) and the **browser-style tab title** at the top. You may keep the **institution footer** (it's your brand).
- Close other apps; **silence notifications**; put AI-PACS **full-screen on the DELL monitor only**; hide the second monitor from the capture region.
- Mouse: move **slowly and deliberately**; pause ~1s between actions; do **3–4 takes** of each beat.

## 2. Camtasia recording settings
- **Recorder:** capture the **AI-PACS monitor only** (custom region = that full screen, 1920×1080).
- **Frame rate:** **60 fps** (keeps stack-scroll and 3D rotation smooth).
- **Cursor:** visible (we want to show interaction); enable cursor smoothing in edit.
- **System audio/mic:** off (we add VO/music later).
- Record each beat as a **separate clip** so takes are easy to pick.

## 3. Getting 9:16 from a landscape capture
- Record **landscape 1080p** (clean, full context). In Camtasia, drop the clip on a **1080×1920 (9:16) canvas** and **scale/position** so the action fills the frame:
  - For the **3D beat**, crop tight on the **3D pane** (top-right quadrant) — it's near-square and fills 9:16 beautifully.
  - For the **MPR tri-planar**, frame the **axial + one side pane** vertically, or do a slow **pan** across the quadrants.
- Keep a **16:9 master** too (for YouTube/website) — same footage, no crop.

## 4. BEAT 1 — Open → MPR → 3D  (teaser 3–8s; record ~8s, trim to ~5s)
*The credibility + "wow" beat.*

**Setup:** anonymized **CT** study; be on the **viewer** with the soft-tissue series ready in the Series Thumbnails panel.

**Take (slow, ~8s):**
1. In the **Series Thumbnails** panel (left), **click the CT series** (e.g., "Tissue") to load it into the viewport. *(0.5s)*
2. Small beat — let the axial image settle. *(0.5s)*
3. Click the **MPR** button (top toolbar, right side). *(0.5s)*
4. **Hold** ~1.5s as the **tri-planar (Axial / Sagittal / Coronal) + 3D** render appears. *(this is the hero moment)*
5. **Left-drag slowly inside the 3D pane** (top-right) to **rotate the volume** ~20–30°. *(2–3s)*
6. Optional: **drag the green crosshair** in the axial pane a little so the other planes update together. *(1s)*

**What to crop for 9:16:** start on the tri-planar (all four quadrants visible), then push/crop into the **3D pane** during the rotation so the clip ends on the rotating 3D.

> Tip: if the 3D rotation reads too fast, slow your drag; you can also slow it 50% in edit.

## 5. BEAT 2 — Scroll → Window/Level → Measure  (teaser 8–12s; record ~8s, trim to ~4s)
*Shows real reading interaction.*

**Setup:** a CT (or MR) series loaded in a single viewport (2D, not MPR).

**Take (slow, ~8s):**
1. **Scroll the mouse wheel** over the image to move through the **stack** — 8–12 slices, smooth. *(2s)*
2. Select the **Window/Level** tool (toolbar) → **drag in the image** (left-right = window, up-down = level). Make one clear contrast change; the **W/L value in the corner overlay updates**. *(2s)*
3. Select the **measurement / ruler** tool → **click two points** across a structure to draw a **distance measurement** with its mm value. *(2s)*
4. Hold the final frame ~1s.

**What to crop for 9:16:** center the crop on the area of action (the cursor + measurement). Keep the **W/L overlay** in frame for one beat so the changing numbers are visible (then blur identifiers separately).
